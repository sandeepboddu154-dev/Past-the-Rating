# Archivra Reach

There is no secret "perfect algorithm" trick. YouTube recommends a video to more people when the people who already saw it
**click** (CTR), **keep watching** (average view duration and percentage viewed) and **are satisfied** (likes, shares,
comments, survey answers, watching more videos, coming back to the channel). The system tests every video on a small audience
and widens it when these signals beat what that audience usually does. So the job is:
**the right topic → packaging that gets clicked → a script that keeps people watching (`archivra-script-doctor`) → a next video to send them to.**

## Mode A — Diagnose a video or the channel

Get the numbers from the user, or from vidIQ / YouTube Studio if available: impressions, CTR, average view duration,
average % viewed, traffic sources, and the retention graph. Since 2026-08-27 YouTube counts a view from the first frame, so
long-form views went up about 1.7–1.9×. Compare videos using **engaged views**, not raw views.

| What you see | The real problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Few impressions | The topic has little demand, or the audience is unclear | Mode B topic work; stay in a narrower niche so the system learns who to show it to |
| Many impressions, low CTR | Packaging | Mode C: new title or thumbnail, A/B test them |
| Good CTR, viewers leave in the first 30 s | The opening does not deliver what the title promised | Rewrite the cold open; match the opening frame to the thumbnail |
| Steady drop in the middle | Pacing, too few re-hooks | `archivra-script-doctor` |
| Good retention, few subscribers | No series identity, no hand-off | Series naming, an end screen pointing to one specific video, playlists |
| Mostly "Browse" or "Suggested" traffic | Good, the system is pushing it | Make more on the same topic or in the same format soon |
| Mostly "Search" traffic | An evergreen asset | Keep the title and description searchable; make related videos |

Always end with the **one** change that will matter most next.

## Mode B — Topic selection

Score each idea from 1–5 on:
1. **Curiosity gap:** can the idea be said as a question people want answered?
2. **Proven demand:** have similar videos done well, especially on channels of similar size? (Outliers are videos with far more views than that channel normally gets.)
3. **Archivra fit:** does it match what current viewers already watch? A new subscriber should want the next video too.
4. **Visual material:** are public-domain or properly licensed archive photos, documents, maps or footage available?
5. **Freshness hook:** an anniversary, something in the news, a new discovery, or a film or series on the subject.

Recommend the top 3 and give a working title for each.
If vidIQ has credits: `vidiq_outliers`, `vidiq_keyword_research` and `vidiq_youtube_video_idea`. Say what each costs first.

## Mode C — Packaging (title + thumbnail together)

Titles: write 10 options, at least 2 from each of these angles:
- **Mystery:** "The ___ No One Could Explain"
- **Reversal:** "Everything You Know About ___ Is Wrong"
- **Stakes / extremes:** "The Most ___ ___ in History"
- **Specific detail:** a strange object, number or date
- **Story:** "How One ___ ___"

Rules: put the most important words in the first ~40 characters, aim for under ~60 characters total, make it accurate
(clickbait that the video doesn't deliver hurts retention and satisfaction), and don't repeat the thumbnail text.
Pick the top 3 and explain why.

Thumbnail concepts (give 3):
- One focal subject (a face, object or place) that is readable on a phone at the size of a stamp.
- At most 3–4 words of text, and they should add to the title rather than repeat it.
- High contrast; a consistent Archivra look (colour, font, a frame or "archive file" style) so returning viewers recognise it.
- Show the moment of mystery or tension, not a calm overview.
- Upload 2–3 versions to YouTube's built-in "Test & compare".

Description: the first 2 lines repeat the promise and include the main search phrase. Then chapters (taken from the script
or a Whisper transcript), sources and archive credits (this builds trust), and links to 2 related Archivra videos or a playlist.
Tags matter very little, so add a few and move on.

## Mode D — Shorts and the funnel

- Every long video should produce 3–5 Shorts: the strongest moments, re-cut vertically with captions.
- Each Short should be complete in itself, with a hook in the first 2 s and an ending that loops. On YouTube, use the
  "related video" link to the long video.
- Post the same clips to Instagram Reels with a "Comment ARCHIVE" call to action and an automatic DM that sends viewers to the long video.
- Shorts bring in viewers who will find you; long videos earn watch time and loyal subscribers. Do both.

## Mode E — Consistency

- Publish on a schedule you can keep. One good long video plus several Shorts a week beats rushed daily uploads.
- Name series (for example "Archivra Files #12") and group them into playlists to increase watching several videos in a row.
- In the first hour after publishing, reply to comments and pin a question that invites answers.
- After 48 h and 7 days, check CTR and retention with Mode A. If CTR is low, change the thumbnail or title. The video is not finished yet.

Never recommend buying views or subscribers, sub-for-sub schemes, misleading titles or reusing other creators' footage
without permission. These hurt the satisfaction signals and can lead to strikes.
