# Deliverables 2 & 3 — Niche Research, Comparison Matrix, and Decision

## Method and honesty notes
- **Demand evidence:** vidIQ channel search (25 channels in the engineering-failure/documentary space, live metrics as of 15 Nov 2026) and vidIQ Shorts outlier data (25 breakout Shorts, last ~3 months). The full tables are in `03-competitor-research.md`.
- **RPM figures are industry-reported ranges, not measurements.** RPM depends heavily on audience country (a US/UK viewer is often worth 5–15× an Indian viewer for ads), video length (mid-rolls at 8+ min) and season (Q4 is highest). Treat them as relative, not as promises. Our real RPM is unknown until we're monetized.
- Scores are my judgement from 1 (bad) to 10 (best). For **Competition**, 10 means *least* crowded.
- Weights: RPM 20%, Viral 15%, Competition 10%, Demand 15%, Production feasibility 15%, Scalability 10%, Monetization opportunities 15%.

### Why the others lost (the hard calls)

- **Business & money exposed (#2), Hidden business stories (#5), Psychology of money (#6).** You asked me to investigate these specifically. They have the best raw RPM, but they're among the most saturated faceless categories on YouTube (Company Man, Modern MBA, Magnates Media, Bright Sun Films, Business Casual, plus thousands of AI-voiced clones). A brand-new channel competes there on volume and voice alone, which is exactly what YouTube's 2026 inauthentic-content enforcement targets. And the name "Past The Rating" fits them awkwardly. **I am not rejecting the money angle. I'm absorbing it as a layer** (below), where it's a differentiator instead of a crowded lane.
- **Personal finance India (#7).** Huge demand and affiliate potential, but it carries credibility and regulatory risk (SEBI's finfluencer rules), Indian ad RPM is low, and we have no credentials to lead with. Feasibility is too low.
- **AI & tech (#3).** It decays fast: last month's AI news is worthless next month. That works against building a library that earns for years.
- **Aviation analysis (#12).** Credibility-gated. Mentour Pilot-style expert channels own it.
- **Human body limits (#9).** Great Shorts virality, weak advertisers. **Kept as a sub-pillar** because it feeds the Shorts funnel.

## Decision: ONE primary niche

> **Engineering limits: the numbers things are rated for, what happens past them, and the decisions and money behind them.**

### Business rationale
1. **The channel name is a moat.** "Past The Rating" literally describes the niche. Competitors use generic names ("Disaster Channel", "Iron Brain Hub"). Ours explains itself and is ownable.
2. **Proven demand with breakout room for new channels.** From the vidIQ data: channels launched in 2026 are breaking out (Mido Explained, launched May 2026, 15.5K subs, 7.9M views; Lost War Machines, May 2026, views +376% in 30 days; The Hydraulic Record, June 2026, **360K average views on just 15 videos**). Breakout Shorts come from tiny channels: X-Ray Builds (1.5K subs, 62.9K views), Oddorizon (37 subs, 15.3K views), ShadowFrame (1.6K subs, 30.7K views, 22× breakout).
3. **Evergreen library.** A 1981 collapse or a 1995 wing test is as watchable in 2030 as today. Every long-form video keeps earning watch hours and, later, ad revenue.
4. **Retention-native.** Every story has built-in tension (the number climbing toward the limit) and a payoff (did it hold?). That's the strongest retention structure on YouTube.
5. **We can raise RPM deliberately** with two levers that most disaster channels ignore:
   - **The money/decision layer** (who decided, what it cost, who paid) pulls business, insurance, legal and B2B engineering advertisers.
   - **"Read the Rating"** consumer episodes (IP68 phones, tyre speed ratings, crash-test stars, helmet certifications) are high-intent consumer topics with strong advertisers and natural affiliate links.
6. **Production feasibility with zero budget.** Diagrams, numbers and motion graphics can be built for free (we already rendered brand assets locally). US-government investigation reports (NBS/NIST/NTSB) are public domain and visually rich.
7. **Sponsor-friendly.** Engineering education channels attract sponsors like Brilliant, CuriosityStream/Nebula, engineering software, tools and VPN/insurance, once we have an audience.

### The risk I'm accepting
- Base RPM is lower than pure finance. We offset this with the money layer, the consumer pillar, long-form mid-rolls, and a Western-weighted audience via English narration and US/UK-relevant topics.
- Tragedy content can get "limited ads". Mitigation: forensic and respectful tone, no graphic imagery, no death counts in titles or thumbnails.
